## 1. Verdict

The cards are a usable coaching tool. Each plan line names one pitch, one count, sometimes the hand, and how far off it was, in plain language. The target allows for leaks that shrink on their own, and the card says honestly where a pitch is "one for the video room, not a take rule". What stops me handing them out as they are is that the documents too often disagree: notes that change the pitch or the hand, two staff reports that disagree about Neto, numbering that reads backwards, a "No target" line under a 28-point gap, and a video list with left-handers inside "vs RHP" leaks. Make the documents agree and fix how the plan is printed, and these become the off-season handout. Until then a coach has to reconcile every packet before a hitter sees it.

## 2. Scores

- **Clarity: 3.** The plan sentences are excellent. Three run figures in the header (11.7, −13.5, "14 runs"), an unexplained xChase+, "points above the league" targets and ②-before-① numbering drag it down.
- **Actionability: 4.** Every line is a take rule a hitter can carry to the plate, and the CSV has game, at-bat and pitch ids for pulling clips. The hand mismatches and a target nobody can track until the in-season readout exists (waiting) hold it back.
- **Consistency: 2.** Note vs plan, report vs report, roster vs card, and the CSV's leak names vs its own hand column all disagree somewhere (Issues 1-5, 8 and 12).
- **Visual design: 4.** Clean and to scale, with "Inside/Away" labelled. Print page 2's "What normal looks like, with two strikes" is the best teaching panel in the packet. Against that: an overlap bug on 2 of 12 prints, "-0.0" on 4 of 12, switch-hitter orientation, and the roster's key columns cut off at desktop width.
- **Coaching value: 4.** Where he chases, which pitch, how far off, choice vs contact, and a target that accounts for regression is what a staff wants. The reports add scouting detail the cards lack, such as velocity and pitch types.
- **Trust: 3.** The honesty ("hits come later, if they come", video items, no target where none applies) builds trust. A hitter who catches "above the belt" against "above the zone", or a lefty in his "vs RHP" reel, stops trusting the rest.

## 3. Strengths

- **The plan line is written like a coach.** It names the count, the pitch, "let it go", the rate against the league and "typically N inches off". It always ends with "Keep swinging at strikes".
- **Fixes are kept small.** One pitch per line, at most two lines, and "The only changes on this card are the pitches above."
- **Borderline pitches aren't made into rules.** Loftin's card and Peña's README entry say "one for the video room, not a take rule".
- **Contact hitters get a different message.** Montgomery and Gorman get "Your bigger issue is contact", plus a "Misses on chases" row on the interactive card.
- **The target is honest.** It lays out expected regression, and print page 2 says "Under it is a fix. Matching it is not."
- **Print page 2 teaches.** "Nobody is judged for a two-strike swing everyone makes" and the average-hitter vs you panel are the right first conversation.
- **Some notes add real coaching intelligence.** Neto: "pitchers threw him far more (290, up from 184)". Clement: weak contact that never looks like a mistake.
- **The video list is pullable.** leak_pitches_2026.csv has date, pitcher, hand, count, pitch, mph, inches off, result and game, at-bat and pitch ids.
- **The roster's "Start with" column** gives a one-line agenda per hitter.
