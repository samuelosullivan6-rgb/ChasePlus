# 1. Verdict

The plan lines are the best part of the card. They give count, pitch, location, how far off and "let it go" in a hitter's own words, and on the plain cards (Caballero, Martínez, Basallo) I'd know what to do in the cage tomorrow. Around those lines the card keeps contradicting itself, and a player alone has no one to settle it:
- plan numbers run out of order on 4 of 11 plans;
- the staff note changes or narrows the instruction on 3 of the 4 noted cards;
- targets give no unit;
- "already at the league's rate" sits under 71% vs 43%;
- "your fastball decisions are a strength" sits beside a fastball fix.

Good chasers get a card that only looks for faults, and the contact-first cards give a take rule for a problem the card says it does not score.

# 2. Scores

- **Clarity: 3.** Plan sentences are plain and concrete. The target line, the cost headline, xChase+, the out-of-order numbering and the web table are not.
- **Actionability: 3.** Each plan line names the count, pitch, spot and distance, which is cage-ready. On 4 of 11 plans the first step is ambiguous, Loftin's card gives no action in the box, and the contact cards' "clearly off" rule is vague.
- **Consistency: 2.** The note disagrees with the plan, the circle numbers disagree with the order, the target disagrees with the plan rates, print "-0.0" disagrees with web "0.0", and leak markers sit on different pictures in print and web.
- **Visual design: 3.** The print page has clean hierarchy, large readable heat maps and the catcher's view is labeled. Against that: markers sit on the wrong picture on 6 of 12 print pages, outlined red areas carry no number on 9 of 12, the footer overprints the chart on 2, and the web table is dense.
- **Coaching value: 3.** Fixes are specific and right-sized, and "Keep swinging at strikes" guards against going passive. Good hitters get nothing to protect, and contact-first hitters get a rule the card can't measure.
- **Trust: 2.** "-0.0", "those spots get better on their own", "no target" beside large gaps, a staff note talking about me in the third person, and "Built by step 17 of the Chase Leaderboard project" all read as unfinished or not written for me.

# 5. Top 3 changes

1. **Make the plan read in order on print and web.**
   - Number the plan lines 1, 2 in working order; don't reuse leak ranks as the plan's circles.
   - Where there's a lead bullet (the contact cards, Diaz), either say which line comes first or fold the bullet into line 1.
   - Box the web picture that matches plan line 1, and put by-hand markers on a by-hand picture in print too.
2. **Make every block agree with plan line 1.**
   - Write the coach's note in the plan's words, for a reader who will see it: "above the zone," not "above the belt," and no "sit fastball" unless the plan says it.
   - Print "your fastball decisions are a strength" only when no plan line is a fastball.
   - Settle Loftin's card: either a take rule with a target, or no target.
   - Never print "already at the league's rate" under a plan line with a large gap.
3. **Rewrite the target and headline for a player.**
   - Name the pitches and the unit, for example "on these two pitches you chased 24 points more than the league; get it under 14 points."
   - Say plainly that most hitters drift back some and he has to beat that.
   - Drop "-0.0".
   - For good chasers, lead with a line like "You're 40th of 261 at laying off; one small thing."
